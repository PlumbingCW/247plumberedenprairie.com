Water heater installation in Eden Prairie covers more than setting a tank on a pan. It includes the fuel choice, the vent path, the water connections and the safety devices that protect your house, and getting those decisions right at the planning stage saves rework later.

This page is for planned jobs: a new build, a relocation, a fuel conversion or an upgrade you are choosing on your own schedule. Our 24/7 dispatch line takes calls any time, but planned installs are booked for convenient appointments. If a tank has already failed, start with water heater replacement.

Fuel choices: gas, electric or heat pump hybrid

Gas heaters recover fast and cost less to run in many homes, but they need a flue and combustion air. Electric tank heaters are simple, with no venting, but they draw from a dedicated circuit and recover more slowly.

Heat pump hybrids pull heat from the surrounding air, so they need clearance, airflow and a place to drain condensate. They also cool and dehumidify the space they sit in, which is a plus in a basement and a minus in a small closet. The right pick depends on your gas service, panel capacity and where the unit will live.

Combustion air matters as much as the flue. A gas unit in a sealed utility room can starve for air, which causes poor flames, soot and carbon monoxide. We size the room or add air openings or a sealed-combustion unit as needed.

Thermal expansion and pressure control

Heated water expands. In a closed system, with a backflow preventer or pressure reducing valve on the service line, that extra volume has nowhere to go, and pressure spikes can trip the T&P valve or shorten fixture life.

An expansion tank absorbs the pressure rise. A pressure reducing valve evens out high incoming pressure so fixtures and the tank are not stressed. Both are inexpensive protection compared to a ruined tank, and we check them on every install.

Connections and safety fittings

Good installs use flexible stainless connectors or copper with dielectric fittings to separate dissimilar metals, a full-port shutoff valve above the tank, and a drain pan with a drain line where a leak could damage finished space. The T&P discharge pipe must run to a safe location, never capped or reduced.

Gas lines get a drip leg and a union, with every joint tested for leaks. Electric units get the right breaker, wire gauge and a disconnect where required.

Condensate, drain pans and basements

High-efficiency gas units and heat pump hybrids produce condensate. It drains by gravity to a floor drain or by a small pump when the unit sits below the drain, and some condensate is acidic enough to need a neutralizer.

Most Eden Prairie homes date from the 1970s onward, and the water meter is usually in the basement near the heater, so the heater, softener, meter and sump often share a small mechanical area. Mapping those items before the install avoids surprises. Pairing the project with water softener and filtration work is easy when the layout is planned.

The water you are heating

The City softens its tap water with lime to about 5 grains per gallon, so scale builds slowly in a new heater here compared with towns that rely on unsoftened wells. A yearly flush and an anode rod check still protect the investment. We cover descaling and flow requirements for on-demand units on the tankless water heater page.

Code and permit questions

Plumbing work in Minnesota follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code (Minnesota Rules chapter 4714). Whether your job needs a permit varies, so ask the Eden Prairie building department. The City's Building Inspections Division issues the Sewer and Water Permit, and a permit becomes void if the authorized work is not started within 180 days.

We will tell you what to ask and plan the visit around any inspection timing.

Choosing capacity

Capacity follows household habits. Count how many fixtures run in the morning peak, whether you have a large soaking tub, and how many people shower back to back.

A first-hour rating on the unit's label is more useful than gallons alone. Undersized tanks run out of hot water, and oversized tanks waste standby heat. If you are adding bathrooms or a kitchen, consider a bigger plan under plumbing installation.

Signs it is time to plan an install

Rising age

A tank past eight years deserves a plan before it fails.

Running out fast

Showers go cold midway because the tank is undersized or full of sediment.

Rust-tinged hot water

Reddish hot water points to a corroding tank interior.

Remodel ahead

New bathrooms or a bigger family change the load.

Before your installation: a homeowner checklist

  • Locate the shutoff valve for the water supply and the gas shutoff if you have one.
  • Measure the space, including ceiling height and door width for the new unit.
  • Clear stored items and a path from the stairs.
  • Note the old unit's fuel, size and venting.
  • Decide whether you want a drain pan and a leak alarm.
  • Ask your building department about permits.
