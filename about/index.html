About Eden Prairie 24/7 Plumbing

A 24/7 plumbing dispatch line for Eden Prairie, MN and the towns around it, built around clear prices and plain answers.

24/7 emergency line(952) 866-9120Call now

Eden Prairie 24/7 Plumbing is a dispatch line for plumbing emergencies and planned work in Eden Prairie, Minnesota. You call one number, describe what is happening, and the dispatcher sends a licensed plumber, with same-day appointments available when a slot is open.

We work from 7500 Flying Cloud Dr in Eden Prairie, and the phone is answered around the clock. The city has grown from about 2,000 residents in 1960 to more than 64,000 today, according to the City, and the plumbing in it ranges from 1970s subdivisions to newer builds.

How a call works

The dispatcher asks what you see and hear, where the main shutoff is and whether water is near anything electrical. You get short steps to limit damage, then an appointment. The plumber explains the problem and the price before any work starts, so a surprise on the invoice is not part of the plan.

What we do and what we do not

We handle drains and sewers, water heaters and boilers, leaks and burst pipes, fixtures, sump pumps and commercial plumbing. We do not repair forced-air furnaces or do structural foundation work, and we say so on the phone rather than send someone out for a job that belongs to another trade. The services page lists everything we do.

Where we work

The line covers Eden Prairie and the nearby towns of Minnetonka, Hopkins, Chanhassen and Bloomington. Each town has its own page with local water facts, because what a city does to its water changes what a plumber should check.

Emergencies and planned work

A burst pipe, a sewer backup and a water heater that has quit are emergencies, and the dispatcher treats them that way. A dripping faucet, an old water heater you want to replace before it fails, or a basement you want to keep dry are planned jobs. Planned work still starts with the same phone call, but the conversation is slower.

The dispatcher asks about the home, the fixture and the timing you prefer, and the plumber arrives with the right parts for the job described.

Sorting a call into one of those two groups matters because it changes what you should do in the next ten minutes. With an emergency, the first job is to limit damage, and the plumber's job comes after. With planned work, the first job is to compare options, and a good plumber will lay them out plainly, including the option of waiting.

What the dispatcher asks

Expect a handful of short questions. What is happening, and where in the house? Is water still flowing? Is anything electrical nearby? Can you reach the main shutoff, and does it turn? Is more than one fixture affected? The answers let the dispatcher decide how urgent the visit is and what the plumber should carry.

You do not need to know the cause. Describe what you see, hear and smell, and leave the diagnosis to the plumber. A gurgling toilet, a wet ceiling stain or a water heater that clicks and stays cold are all useful clues.

Why a local line matters

Eden Prairie sits in Hennepin County, where the seasons drive most plumbing calls. Winter brings frozen and split pipes, spring brings sump pumps that run nonstop, and summer storms bring basement water. A dispatcher who works in the same area knows those seasonal patterns and asks about them.

That local knowledge is also why the site has a page for each town we serve. A homeowner in Hopkins and a homeowner in Chanhassen can both call the same number, but the facts that matter to their plumbing, such as the age of nearby housing or whether a property is on city water, are not identical.

What you can expect on a visit

The plumber looks at the problem first, explains what is wrong in plain words, and gives the price before any work begins. If a repair is not worth the cost, for example on a very old fixture, you will hear that too. If the problem turns out to belong to another trade, the plumber will say so and not guess.

Photos of the problem, such as a leaking joint or a model label on a water heater, help when you describe things on the phone. They are optional, and the plumber will check everything in person.

How to check any plumber in Minnesota

The Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry runs a license and registration lookup on its website. It searches by a complete license number, so ask for the number and check it. State rules also require a plumbing contractor to keep a licensed master plumber in charge of its plumbing work, and unlicensed workers must be supervised.
