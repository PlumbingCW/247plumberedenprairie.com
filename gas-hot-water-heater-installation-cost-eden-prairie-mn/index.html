Gas Water Heater Installation Cost in Eden Prairie, MN

Gas water heater costs depend on venting, the gas line, combustion air and drains, and a gas smell always means leaving the house before you pick up the phone.

By the Eden Prairie 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated October 16, 2026

Gas hot water heater installation in Eden Prairie, MN costs more or less depending on four things that electric heaters do not have: a flue, a gas supply, a supply of air for burning, and sometimes a condensate drain. A like-for-like swap is the simple end. Moving the heater, changing the vent path or upsizing the gas line pushes the job toward the other end.

If you ever smell gas, leave the building first.

Gas safety comes before cost

The smell of rotten eggs, a hissing sound near a gas fitting or a pilot that keeps blowing out are all warnings. Leave the building right away and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches, use phones or light flames inside, and do not return until responders say it is safe.

A carbon monoxide alarm that sounds means getting everyone outside and calling 911. Both hazards are why gas heater work is a job for a licensed plumber, with every connection leak-tested before the unit is fired up.

The cost drivers that only gas heaters have

Four items separate gas installs from electric ones, and each shows up as its own line on a good quote.

VentingThe flue must carry exhaust outside and rise correctly, and a new model may need different pipe or a longer run.
Gas line and shutoffThe supply pipe, a shutoff within reach and a sediment trap must suit the new burner.
Combustion airA closet or tight utility room needs enough air to burn fuel safely.
Drain and relief pipingThe pressure relief valve needs a discharge pipe that ends in a safe spot.
CondensateA condensing model makes acidic water that needs a drain or neutralizer.

A heater with an atmospheric vent uses a metal flue that rises on its own, which keeps equipment simple. Power-vent and condensing models use fans and plastic pipe that can run sideways through a wall. They are more efficient, but they ask for electricity, a drain and clear space outside.

Gas line size and the shutoff

Gas heaters pull a lot of fuel when the burner is on. If the old heater was small and you step up in size, or if you add a furnace or range on the same line, the pipe may be undersized. A plumber checks pipe diameter and length against the new unit's rating.

Every gas appliance needs a shutoff valve within reach, and a drip leg, which is a short capped pipe that catches debris before it reaches the burner. If either is missing, the installer adds it. Joints get tested with a leak solution or a pressure gauge, not by smell.

Combustion air and the closet problem

A gas burner takes air from the room and sends exhaust up the flue. In a small closet with a louvered door or a sealed basement room, the heater can run short of air, burn poorly and back-draft exhaust. Tight modern homes make this more likely, because they leak less air than older ones.

The fix may be louvers, a duct to the outside or a different location, and each adds cost. A sealed-combustion model draws its air from outdoors through a pipe, which sidesteps the problem in tight spaces.

Condensate, drains and the old tank

Condensing units and many high-efficiency models drip acidic water as they run. That water needs a floor drain, a pump or a neutralizer cartridge, and the installer may run a small line to a laundry drain.

The relief valve also needs a drain. The pipe must end where hot water cannot hurt anyone, and it cannot be capped or plugged. Hauling away the old heater and sealing the old vent opening are part of the job too. Ask for these lines separately on the quote for water heater installation and replacement.

Code, permits and who to ask

Gas water heater work sits under the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and under local permit rules. We do not list the code requirements here, because they depend on the model and the house. A licensed plumber knows how to size the vent, test the gas line and set the relief piping. Local note

Eden Prairie local note: soft tap water and licensed help

The City of Eden Prairie says it softens its water with lime at the treatment plant. Untreated well water averages roughly 21 grains per gallon, and the tap averages about 5 grains per gallon. Less scale builds up inside a tank heater than with untreated hard water, but a tank still collects sediment, so flushing and anode checks still matter.

On licensing, the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry says a plumbing contractor must have a licensed master plumber as its responsible individual and must keep a bond and insurance. DLI's lookup searches by the full license ID, so ask for it before you hire.

How long a gas tank lasts and when to replace it

A gas tank heater typically lasts roughly eight to twelve years. Signs of trouble are rust at the base, water around the tank, rumbling sounds from sediment, a flame that burns yellow instead of blue and soot around the draft hood. Any of these is a reason to have it inspected.

Scale shortens life where water is hard. Because the City's tap water averages about 5 grains per gallon after lime softening, scale builds more slowly here, and a regular flush and an anode rod check can help the tank reach the upper end of its range.

Questions worth asking at the quote

Ask for the model, the vent type and where the vent will terminate outside. Ask about the gas line, the shutoff and the sediment trap. Ask who removes the old unit and who pulls the permit. A thorough installer will also test combustion draft and look for leaks after firing the unit, and will show you the result.

Water heater installation follows the manufacturer's instructions and the standards of the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
