Commercial Plumber in the Golden Triangle, Eden Prairie, MN

The Golden Triangle is Eden Prairie's large business district, not a residential neighborhood. This page is for building owners, tenants and property managers with office, industrial and mixed-use plumbing needs.

  • Business and industrial district, commercial plumbing only
  • Eden Prairie, MN

The Golden Triangle is a business and industrial district, so this page is about commercial plumbing rather than homes. Eden Prairie Local News describes it as a roughly 1,000-acre triangular area bounded by Interstate 494 on the south, U.S. Highway 169 on the east and U.S. Highway 212 on the west, with its north end just south of Minnesota Highway 62.

The same reporting says it held about 25,000 jobs before the pandemic and that many of its buildings date from the late 1970s and 1980s.

The district is changing. The City's economic development manager expected in 2024 that older buildings would eventually be replaced, and a 2025 report describes a shift toward more housing near the light rail stations. One 425-unit apartment complex at 6901 Flying Cloud Drive was under construction in 2024. City West, near the northern end of the district beside the light rail station, is a master-planned area of offices, showrooms, townhomes, apartments and a Shady Oak Road retail strip.

Building types and who is responsible

In a district this large, the right plumbing question is rarely "which neighborhood" and almost always "which building and who controls it." A tenant may report the leak while the owner holds the building shutoff and a property manager holds the keys. Multi-tenant office and light-industrial buildings can share risers, drain lines and a single water service across several suites.

Office space

Restrooms, break rooms and a few water heaters. Leaks often show up in a ceiling below.

Light industrial and showrooms

Floor drains, utility sinks, restroom groups and larger water heaters with high-ceiling access.

Mixed-use and apartments

Residential-style fixtures and shared stacks, with association or owner control of shutoffs.

When a suite is rebuilt for a new tenant, plumbing changes such as added sinks, moved restrooms or a new water heater are held to the Minnesota Plumbing Code. Ask the building owner and the City what permits or reviews apply before work starts, because the answer depends on the building and the scope.

Our commercial plumbing page lists the common problems for businesses, including restroom stoppages, grease lines, backflow testing and water heaters.

Common issues in older commercial buildings

The age of a building tells you less about its pipes than its maintenance history. For a building from the late 1970s or 1980s, ask your property manager whether any water, drain or heater work has been done, and photograph the equipment before a problem starts. Useful checks include:

  • A floor drain or restroom group that backs up together, which can point to a shared line. Our drain cleaning and hydro jetting pages explain how those lines are cleared.
  • A water bill that rises without a change in operations, which suggests a leak. See leak detection.
  • A water heater serving restrooms or a break room that is slow to recover or leaking. See water heater replacement.

Before you call

Know the building address and suite, the type of use, and where the shutoff valves are. If water is escaping, close the nearest zone valve, stop using the affected restrooms and drains, and keep people away from wet floors and electrical panels. Tell the dispatcher if you are the tenant, owner or manager.

Call (952) 866-9120 and ask about service for your address. The Golden Triangle is part of Eden Prairie and our pages for Minnetonka and Hopkins cover the cities beyond Highway 62. Plumbing work in Minnesota follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
