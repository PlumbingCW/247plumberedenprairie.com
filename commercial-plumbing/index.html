A commercial plumbing emergency in Eden Prairie is any leak, backup or failure that stops a business from operating or damages the space, and our 24/7 dispatch line takes those calls. While you wait, shut off the water at the nearest zone valve if you know where it is, stop using the affected restrooms and drains, and keep staff and customers away from wet floors and electrical panels. Tell the dispatcher what type of building it is.

That last detail matters more than most callers expect. A restaurant kitchen, a medical office, a retail strip and a warehouse bathroom all have different piping, different shutoffs and different people responsible for them.

What kind of building is it

A tenant may call about a leak while the owner controls the building shutoffs, and a property manager may hold the keys. Larger buildings have multiple risers and mechanical rooms, and the right shutoff may be two floors away.

What business owners call us about

Restroom stoppages

Several toilets or floor drains back up together, or a single stall will not clear.

Water through a ceiling

A stain or drip under an upper floor points to a leak overhead.

A grease line that smells or backs up

Kitchen drains slow down, and an odor comes from the floor sink or the trap area.

Hot water running out

A water heater or boiler serving a building is cold, noisy or leaking at the base.

A water bill that jumps

Use stays high with no change in operations, which suggests a hidden or running leak.

When a commercial plumbing emergency cannot wait

Treat it as urgent when a leak is active above equipment or inventory, when sewage is coming up through floor drains, or when a water heater or boiler is leaking and the building has no hot water. Shut off what you can reach safely and put out wet floor signs. We handle after-hours and weekend calls for these, and same-day appointments are offered when available for problems that can wait a few hours.

Have an address, a building access contact and the space type ready. If the space is leased, check who is allowed to call a plumber.

Commercial leak detection

Tools include acoustic listening devices, thermal cameras, pressure testing and camera inspection of drain lines. The goal is to find the pipe, not just open walls and hope.

We will be honest about scope. A hidden leak in a large building can involve several floors, shared walls and systems that belong to the owner rather than the tenant. We tell you what can be found, what needs the owner's permission, and what may need a specialist. See our leak detection page for how the process works.

Backflow testing

Backflow testing is planned work, not an emergency. A backflow preventer sits on a water line to keep contaminated water from flowing backward into the potable supply, and the device has to be tested periodically. Rules on who must test, how often and who may perform the test vary, so ask the Eden Prairie building department or Utilities Division which rules apply to your building.

A failed test may mean repair or replacement, so schedule early.

Grease trap cleaning

A grease trap or interceptor catches fats, oils and food solids from kitchen sinks and floor drains before they enter the sewer. Once it fills, grease passes into the line, odors rise and drains slow. Cleaning removes the buildup so the trap can do its job.

Keep records of cleaning dates, since inspectors and landlords may ask. The City's Utilities Division oversees the sanitary sewer, which carries about 262 miles of main to treatment by the Metropolitan Council, so grease that gets past a trap eventually becomes the public system's problem as well.

Storm drain cleaning

Lot and dock catch basins plug with leaves, silt and trash, leaving standing water. They connect to a public system the City describes as over 700 stormwater ponds and 209 miles of pipe. We clear on-site storm drains, and questions about the public side go to the Utilities Division.

Drain and sewer backups

When one fixture backs up, the problem is local. When several do, especially at ground level, the main line is likely blocked. Our drain cleaning crews use cables for soft blockages, and hydro jetting cleans grease and scale from large-diameter lines. A camera inspection shows roots, broken joints and low sags.

If the main outside is the problem, sewer line cleaning is the next step. Ownership of the lateral can be unclear in commercial settings, so ask the Utilities Division who maintains what.

Restrooms, water heaters and boilers

Heavy restroom use wears flushometer valves, urinal flush valves and floor drains quickly. Sticking or constantly running valves waste water and are usually a short fix.

Water heaters and boilers in commercial buildings are larger and more varied than home units. Our water heater repair and boiler repair pages cover the basics, and tell us the model and fuel type when you call. A restaurant or clinic without hot water should say so right away.

Tenant improvement plumbing

A build-out for a new tenant means new restrooms, sinks, floor drains or kitchen lines. Plumbing rough-in comes before walls close, and permits and inspections vary, so check with the Eden Prairie building department. Bring the floor plan and the landlord's requirements.

Business plumbing questions

Commercial plumbing is held to the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, including its standards for backflow protection, grease interceptors and fixture counts.

What to do first in a commercial emergency

Shut off the water at the main or the affected branch, keep customers and staff away from wet floors and electrical equipment, and call the 24/7 line. Do not use chemical cleaners on a backed-up line, and do not enter a flooded basement or pit where power may be on.
