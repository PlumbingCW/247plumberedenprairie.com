While you wait, turn off the disposal switch, stop using the sink and dishwasher, set a bucket under any drip, and do not put your hand inside the grinder.

Most disposal problems are not that dramatic. A jam, a hum with no spin, or a tripped reset button usually has a quick fix, and we will tell you honestly when a repair makes sense and when the unit has reached the end of its life.

What the problem usually is

A plumber diagnoses by sound, by water path and by a flashlight look before touching anything.

Common signs your disposal needs attention

A hum but no grinding

The motor has power but the grinding plate cannot turn. Something is wedged against it, often a bone, fruit pit or bottle cap.

Silence when you flip the switch

Check the red reset button on the bottom of the unit first. If it pops back out, the motor overheated or overloaded.

Water under the cabinet

A wet cabinet floor means a leak at the flange, the dishwasher hose connection or the housing itself.

Sink slow to drain or gurgling

Food waste has packed the trap or the line past the disposal, or the dishwasher drain is tied in to a partial clog.

A sour or rotten smell

Food film builds on the grinding chamber and the rubber splash guard, or the drain trap is holding old waste.

Fixing a jammed disposal

Unplug the unit or switch off its breaker before anything else. Most disposals have a hex socket in the center of the underside, and a hex key (often included in the box) lets you turn the plate back and forth until it spins freely. Pull out whatever caused the jam with tongs, never fingers, then wait a few minutes and press the reset button.

If the key will not turn at all, or the unit jams again within days, the shaft or the grinding plate may be damaged. That is the point where repair stops paying off on an older, low-horsepower model.

Leaks: where the water is coming from

Dry everything and run the water again to trace a leak. Water at the top, around the sink flange, means the plumber's putty or the mounting assembly has loosened and can usually be reseated. Water at the side dishwasher inlet is often a loose hose clamp.

Water from the bottom seam or housing means the casing has failed and the unit must be replaced. A leak left alone rots the cabinet floor.

When a disposal leak or backup is an emergency

Treat it as urgent when water is running steadily from the unit, when the sink and dishwasher are both pushing dirty water up, or when the leak is near a power outlet. Turn off the disposal at the wall switch, close the valves under the sink if you can reach them without standing in water, and call. We handle after-hours and weekend calls for these, and same-day appointments are offered when available for the rest.

Sink and dishwasher backing up

When both sides of a double sink fill, or the dishwasher leaves a puddle after a cycle, the clog is usually past the disposal. Grease and starch settle in the trap and the horizontal run, and the disposal only grinds food, it does not clear pipe. See our drain cleaning work for how a cable or camera finds the blockage.

If the backup reaches other fixtures, the problem may sit in the main line. In that case a sewer line cleaning visit is the better next step than another disposal repair.

Installing a new disposal

We remove the old unit, clean the sink flange, set the new mounting ring and snap the new body into place, then connect the discharge to the trap.

If a dishwasher is connected, the knockout plug at the new unit's drain inlet must be punched out first, or the dishwasher will not drain. The wiring goes under the cover plate with the ground secured. For a first-time installation, see our plumbing installation page, since the switch and trap may need new work.

Horsepower, feed type and what not to grind

Smaller motors suit one or two people, and larger ones grind tougher scraps with less jamming. Batch-feed models run only with the stopper in place, while continuous-feed units run from a wall switch. We help you pick for your sink and household.

Grease, oil and coffee grounds cause more trouble than bones do. Keep out pasta, rice, eggshell membranes, fibrous celery, corn husks, glass and plastic, and run cold water during and after grinding.

Safety steps before touching a disposal

Switch off the disposal and unplug it or turn off its breaker before you reach into the sink. Never put a hand in the chamber, even if the unit seems dead. Use tongs or pliers to remove a jammed object, and call if the unit is leaking near its electrical connection.
