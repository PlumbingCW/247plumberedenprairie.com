Chanhassen is the neighbor where the water itself changes the plumbing advice. The City of Chanhassen says its groundwater contains naturally occurring iron, manganese and hardness, and that treatment removes nearly all the iron and manganese but does not reduce hardness. Its listed hardness is 21 grains per gallon, and the city does not soften the water.

That is a sharp contrast with Eden Prairie, where the City softens tap water to about 5 grains per gallon. If you moved from one to the other, or you split time between them, your fixtures, heater and softener settings are not interchangeable. Water first: the problems 21 grains per gallon creates

Hard water leaves calcium on faucet aerators, showerheads and the heating elements of electric water heaters. The city's own page notes the spots on dishes and the buildup on fixtures, and says most residents benefit from a home water softener. The practical result is that Chanhassen heaters often fail from sediment and scale before they fail from rust.

Here is what we check on a call about no hot water or a rumbling tank. We flush the tank, test the elements or burner, inspect the anode rod, and look at the temperature and pressure relief valve for crusting. A scaled-up tank may be worth a repair, while a tank with a leaking seam needs replacement.

What to do in the next ten minutes

1

Close the main valve

Turn the main shutoff fully closed. It is usually on the basement wall where the water line enters the house.

2

Cut power to the wet area

Switch off the circuit for a wet utility room or basement only if you can do it from dry ground. If water reaches the panel, stay out and say so on the phone.

3

Move the contents

Lift boxes, rugs and electronics out of the way, and open a low faucet to drain the lines.
