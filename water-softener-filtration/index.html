Water Softener and Filtration in Eden Prairie, MN

The City already softens Eden Prairie tap water, so a home system is not for everyone. Here is who needs treatment, who does not, and how licensed plumbers install and service units.

Before you buy a water softener in Eden Prairie, check what the City already does to your water. The City softens it with lime at its treatment plant, and it says residents do not need a home softener. That changes the question from "which unit should I buy" to "do I have a specific problem that treatment would fix."

Some homes do have one. Private wells, a particular taste or sediment problem, or a single tap where you want cleaner drinking water are all real reasons, and we install and service for them.

What the City does to your water

Eden Prairie's tap water comes from 15 deep City wells in the Jordan-Prairie du Chien aquifer group. Untreated well water averages roughly 21 grains per gallon of hardness, about 360 mg/L. At the plant, the City softens it with lime, and the tap averages about 5 grains per gallon, or about 90 mg/L.

The City describes hardness as mainly a nuisance and not a regulated contaminant. Chlorine is the disinfectant, and fluoride is added as state law requires. If you are on City water, you are starting from softened water already.

Who actually needs treatment

Real cases are narrower than sales pitches suggest.

Homes on private wells

Well water is not softened or disinfected by the City, so hardness, iron or bacteria are yours to test and manage.

A specific taste or sediment issue

If water tastes or smells off, the cause might be an aging water heater, the plumbing or a filter that has outlived its cartridge. Find the cause before buying equipment.

Drinking-water filters

A carbon filter on the cold line improves taste and removes chlorine flavor for people who dislike it.

Reverse osmosis at one tap

A small under-sink reverse osmosis unit treats drinking and cooking water only, which is cheaper to run than treating the whole house.

How to test your tap

Do not rely on a salesperson's kit at your kitchen table. The City warns that some sellers use false claims and scare tactics, and says to install a home treatment system only if a specific need has been identified.

Instead, ask the water utility for its annual water quality report. Then test your tap through a certified lab if you suspect a problem, and compare the result with the report. If your numbers match the City's published range, you probably have no problem a softener would solve.

Cautions the City raises

Local note

Eden Prairie softener cautions

The City says softened water is not recommended for people on sodium-restricted diets, and that salt-softened water is not recommended for houseplants or irrigation. Salt softeners swap calcium for sodium, so a home softener adds sodium to water that was already treated.

In practice, that means if someone in your household is on a sodium-restricted diet, or you water indoor plants from the tap, ask a doctor and plan a bypass or a separate unsoftened outdoor line before installing.

How nearby cities compare

Water varies sharply across the metro, so what is true in Eden Prairie is not true next door. The City of Chanhassen reports untreated city water at about 21 grains per gallon, does not soften it, and says most residents benefit from a home softener. In Bloomington, the City softens with lime from about 19 grains per gallon down to about 5.2, which is close to Eden Prairie, and you can read about our coverage of Bloomington homes.

Chaska's city website advises setting a home softener to 29 grains per gallon. That figure is specific to Chaska, so it is a poor guide for Eden Prairie settings.

Choosing and installing a system

If you do need one, size it to your household and water use, not to a sales chart. Typical choices include a salt-based softener, a whole-house carbon filter, a sediment prefilter and an under-sink reverse osmosis unit. Each has maintenance: salt, cartridges or membranes.

Placement matters. The unit needs a drain for regeneration, an electrical outlet nearby and a bypass valve. We plan a loop with shutoffs on the main line so the system can be serviced without a shutdown, and we keep the hot water line connected sensibly, which matters when a water heater installation or tankless water heater is in the plan. Larger jobs fall under plumbing installation.

Servicing an existing softener

Many calls are about a unit already in the house, and there is nothing wrong with keeping it.

Softener problems we service

Salt bridging

A hard crust forms in the brine tank, leaving a gap beneath. The softener stops getting brine, and water turns hard again.

Brine tank trouble

Mush at the bottom, too much water or a dirty tank can block the pickup tube.

Tired resin

The resin beads wear out over time and stop exchanging minerals effectively.

Control valve faults

A stuck head, a dead display or a missed regeneration cycle can come from a valve or a timer.

Drain line problems

A kinked or clogged drain tubing can let brine back up or flood the floor.

A bridged tank is often fixed by breaking the crust with a broom handle and refilling with the correct salt. Resin and valve work are a plumber's job, and we will tell you if replacement makes more sense than repair.

Mineral spots and cleaning

White crust on a spout or shower door is usually calcium. The City suggests vinegar or a calcium and lime remover for buildup on fixtures. At about 5 grains per gallon, that is a cleaning chore, not an equipment problem, though a stuck valve may need faucet repair.

Softener and filter questions

A softener or filter is connected to the water supply according to the manufacturer's guidelines and the standards of the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
