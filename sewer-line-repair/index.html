Sewer backup emergency: what to do first

A true backup has a pattern: several fixtures fail together and the lowest drain in the house takes the overflow. What you do first decides how much floor and drywall you lose.

Do this first:

  1. Stop all water use, including toilets
  2. Shut off the washer and dishwasher
  3. Keep people and pets away from standing water, which can carry waste
  4. Unplug anything sitting in or near the wet area if you can do it from dry ground
  5. Call, and tell the dispatcher which drains are involved

Do not run a drain cleaner down the line, do not keep flushing to test it, and do not step into water that touches an outlet or a panel.

Signs the sewer line has failed

What a damaged line looks like

Backups in more than one place

A tub and a toilet backing up together point to the main line, not a single trap.

Gurgling when water runs elsewhere

Air pushed past a partial blockage bubbles up through the nearest open trap.

Wet, sunken or unusually green ground

A stripe of soft turf or a small dip over the pipe route can mean a leak or collapse.

Drains that clog soon after cleaning

A cable clears a channel, but a bellied or cracked line holds the debris right back.

Why lines fail in Eden Prairie homes

Per the City, most Eden Prairie homes date from the 1970s onward, which tells you what is underground. Older homes may have cast iron or clay tile that has scaled, cracked or shifted at joints. Newer lines are often plastic, which holds up well but can sag where backfill settled or a joint was set badly.

Roots find any joint that leaks, and a line often fails at a joint, not mid-pipe, so the repair can be shorter than you fear.

How we find the break

Cleaning first, then a look. A cable or jet clears the blockage so a push camera can travel the line. The camera head is self-leveling, so the picture stays upright as it passes bellies, offsets and root masses, and a locator at the surface marks the exact depth and spot.

Our sewer inspection page shows what the findings mean, and hydro jetting is how we clear a line before the camera when scale or grease blocks the view.

No method wins every time. Pipe depth, material, what sits above it and the slope decide.

Cast iron pipe repair

Cast iron lasts a long time and then fails in predictable ways. Scale builds on the inside and narrows the pipe, the bottom of horizontal runs corrodes through, cracks open from settling, and lead-and-oakum or hub joints weep.

When one section is bad, a cut-out and replacement with a modern coupling is common. When the pipe is scaled throughout or leaking in many places, lining or replacing the run is usually wiser than patching every joint. Ask for the camera footage so you can see why the plumber recommends one over the other.

Trenchless pipe lining

Lining pushes a resin-soaked liner into the old pipe, inflates it and cures it into a new pipe inside the old one. It avoids most digging and suits pipes that are cracked, leaking at joints or coated with roots but still round and aligned.

It will not fix a collapsed pipe, a deep belly that holds water, or a line with a sharp offset. Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through as it breaks the old one, which handles more damage but needs entry and exit pits. Open trench still gives the best look at conditions you cannot see from inside.

Main sewer cleaning

If the main is blocked but the pipe is sound, you may need only cleaning, not repair. A cable with a cutter head or a jetter clears roots, grease and paper, and our sewer line cleaning page covers that route. Day-to-day branch lines are handled under drain cleaning.

Sewer or septic: which system is your property on?

A sewer property connects to a public main that carries waste away. A septic property treats waste on site through a tank and a drain field. Septic needs tank pumping and a field that can saturate, while a sewer connection has a line that can crack, root or belly.

If you are not sure which you have, look for a lid or manhole in the yard, check your records from the home purchase, or ask the City. Symptoms of both systems can look the same at the drain, so identify the system before anyone digs.

Local note

Who handles which stretch in Eden Prairie

The City's Utilities Division oversees water, sanitary sewer and storm sewer. It does not state who maintains a home's sewer service line, so ask the Utilities Division who maintains the section in question before you dig or line it.

The City's Building Inspections Division issues a Sewer and Water Permit whose application covers repair-only work, with rough-in visual, final, meter and air test inspections listed on the form. Whether your job needs one varies, so ask the Eden Prairie building department first. Call 811 before any excavation.
