Neighborhood first: three kinds of Bloomington property, three kinds of call

Post-war single-family homes. Bloomington grew from about 9,900 residents in 1950 to about 50,500 in 1960 and about 82,000 in 1970, according to Census counts. Many of those early homes were built in a low-density pattern, and some began life with private wells and septic systems before city services arrived. That history means older lines, patched connections and basements with decades of drain work behind them.

Townhomes and condos. Shared stacks and shared walls make a leak in one unit a problem for three. Here we spend more time on locating the source, which is where leak detection earns its keep.

Commercial corners. The Mall of America opened in 1992, and the offices and restaurants around it keep commercial plumbing busy with grease lines, restroom backups and after-hours leaks.

Water from two sources

The city blends its own well water with finished water bought from Minneapolis, then softens it with lime, so scale is lighter than you might expect.

Meters in the basement

What Bloomington's water report tells a plumber

The City of Bloomington's water quality report says its supply comes from six deep wells plus a connection to Minneapolis, and the two are blended. The city uses lime softening to bring hardness from about 19 grains per gallon down to about 5.2, and it has inventoried its water services and found no lead service lines.

For you, that means a softer tap than Chanhassen's and about the same as Eden Prairie's. A home softener is rarely needed, and scale in a heater is more likely from age than from water. The city also says it handles water meter repairs, so a suspect meter is a call to the city, while everything on your side of the meter is plumbing.

Sewer backups: the Bloomington specifics

Bloomington publishes a 24-hour sewer backup line of its own. If sewage is coming up from a floor drain, call that city line to report it, and call us for the cleanup and the repair on your side. Do not run water, laundry or dishwashers while you wait.

We start with sewer line cleaning at the cleanout and a camera to see whether the problem is roots, a belly or a collapse. If the line needs work, sewer line repair options are laid out so you can compare them before anyone digs.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak in Bloomington

A quick checklist for the minutes before help arrives

1

Find the meter and close the valve

The meter is usually in the basement near where the copper line comes up through the floor. Close the valve on the house side first.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Turn off breakers feeding wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry ground, and never touch a switch while standing in water.

3

Clear the floor

Lift boxes, rugs and electronics, then open a low faucet so the lines drain.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Say what failed, where the valve is, and whether water is touching electrical equipment. For a sewer backup, also call the city's backup line.
