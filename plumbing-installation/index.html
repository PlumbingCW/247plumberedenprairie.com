Plumbing installation in Eden Prairie usually starts with a floor plan and a list of fixtures, not a leak. Whatever the project, the order of work matters: drains and vents first, supply lines next, inspections in between, and fixtures last. Our 24/7 dispatch line also takes urgent calls, but this page is about planned work.

Good installation is mostly planning. Where the drain runs, how far the vent must travel and where the shutoffs go decide whether the finished room works or annoys you for years.

Where the work usually starts

Most installations fall into a few groups. A kitchen update moves the sink or adds a dishwasher, and a bath remodel swaps a tub for a shower. A finished basement adds a bathroom or wet bar, and a laundry room gets a new box and drain. Each needs its own trap, vent path and shutoff valves.

Signs a patch will not be enough

Walls open already

If framing is exposed, adding or moving lines costs less now than after drywall.

Fixtures moving

Relocating a sink or toilet changes the drain slope and vent path, not just the visible parts.

Old shutoffs that fail

Stop valves that will not close mean new valves belong in the plan.

A basement with no drain

Waste has to be lifted with an ejector pump before any finished bath is possible.

Supply lines and PEX

Cross-linked polyethylene, or PEX, is the common choice for new residential supply lines today. It bends around corners, resists freezing damage better than rigid copper, and uses fewer fittings. A home-run layout runs a separate line from a central manifold to each fixture, so shutting off one sink does not cut water to the shower.

PEX still needs protection. It must be supported at proper intervals, kept away from direct sun, and routed with enough room for expansion. We also cover repiping for older homes where original supply pipes are being replaced instead of added to.

Drain, waste and vent basics

A drain works because air can enter behind the water. Each fixture drains through a trap, which holds a plug of water that blocks sewer gas, and each trap connects to a vent that lets air in so the water does not siphon out. Slope matters too: too flat and solids settle, too steep and water outruns the solids.

Toilets need larger drain pipe and a closer vent, and the floor often has to be opened to run it. The Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, governs this work, and your plumber and inspector read it together.

Basement bathrooms and ejector pumps

Basement fixtures often sit below the city sewer line, so waste cannot drain by gravity. A sewage ejector pump collects waste in a sealed basin and lifts it up to the house drain. Choose a pump sized to the fixtures, and plan the basin, the vent and the access cover before concrete is cut.

If the basement also has a sump pit, keep the two separate. Our sump pump repair page explains why groundwater and sewage never share a basin.

Permits and inspections

Local note

Eden Prairie permits and inspections

Eden Prairie's Building Inspections Division issues the City's Sewer and Water Permit. The form covers sewer, water, commercial storm, inside water meters and repair-only work. Required inspections listed on that form are the rough-in visual, the final, the meter and the air test.

A permit becomes void if the authorized work is not started within 180 days, so schedule your plumber before you pay. Whether a particular job needs a permit varies, so ask the Eden Prairie building department before you open a wall.

Hiring a licensed contractor

Minnesota's Department of Labor and Industry says a plumbing contractor must always have a licensed master plumber as its responsible individual and must maintain a bond and insurance. Employees without a license work under direct supervision. You can check any plumber with the Department's license and registration lookup, which searches by the full license ID, so ask for that ID in writing before you hire.

State law lets a person without a plumber's license do plumbing work that meets the Plumbing Board's minimum standards on a home they own and live in. A local ordinance can forbid it, so ask the City first. Even where it is allowed, mistakes are expensive to open up and redo.

Gas smell safety comes first

If you smell gas, leave the building first. Do not flip switches, use a phone or light a flame inside. From outside, call the gas utility or 911, and only then call a plumber. We never promise a response time for gas calls.

Gas piping work is a separate matter from water and drain work. Who may install or inspect gas lines and what permits apply varies, so ask when you call. If you are planning a gas line for a range, dryer or water heater, we can tell you what we can do ourselves and what needs another licensed trade.
