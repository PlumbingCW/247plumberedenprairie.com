Finding the main line and its cleanout

Every house has one pipe that collects all the branches and carries them out toward the street. Where it leaves the foundation you usually find a cleanout, a capped pipe about the size of a coffee can, sometimes in the basement near a floor drain and sometimes outside. That cap is the door a cable machine or jetter uses.

If you cannot find one, tell us. Without a cleanout, the plumber may have to pull a toilet or work through a roof vent, which takes longer and changes the plan.

Is it the main line? Signs to look for

Signs the blockage is in the main

Several fixtures slow together

A tub, a sink and a toilet failing at once point to a shared pipe, not a single trap.

Basement floor drain gurgles or rises

The floor drain sits lowest, so it is the first to show water when the main stops.

Toilet bubbles when the washer drains

Washer water pushes air through the main, and the nearest open trap burps.

Backup follows a heavy water day

Laundry, showers and a dishwasher together can overwhelm a partly blocked pipe.

Sewer smell in the lowest room

Water carrying gas is pushed past the trap seal.

Sewer backup emergency: while you wait

A backup in the main line is a hazard because the water is not clean. Use no water at all, including flushing. Keep children and pets out of the wet area, lift stored boxes off the floor, and if water is reaching outlets, appliances or the panel, stay clear and tell the dispatcher.

Do not pour a chemical cleaner down the line and do not run a plunger at a main backup. A plunger moves the problem, and chemicals sit in standing water. Same-day appointments are available for slower main problems, and we dispatch after hours for a rising backup.

Cable machine or jetter: which tool fits

A sectional cable machine with a cutter head is the traditional tool for a main line. The head spins on the end of the cable, cutting through roots, breaking plugs and pulling out paper. It is the right first tool for a hard blockage or a root mass.

A jetter uses pressurized water to scour the walls and flush grease and scale, and it leaves a cleaner pipe. Hydro jetting fits repeat blockages and greasy lines. Many visits use both, then a camera to confirm the result.

Wipes labeled flushable behave like cloth in a pipe. They catch on a root or a rough joint and gather paper behind them until the line stops. Roots are different: they come back unless the opening is repaired, and our sewer line repair page covers the options.

Why chemical cleaners are a bad idea on a main

A main line holds a lot of water, so a chemical cleaner gets diluted and does little to a root mass or a wipe snag. What it can do is sit in standing water, soften old pipe joints and put a hazard in front of the person who opens the cleanout. If you have already used one, tell the plumber so they can wear the right protection.

Sewer drain cleaning for branches and floor drains

Not every backup is the main. A branch line serving a bathroom group or a laundry can fill up on its own, and that is a smaller job handled under drain cleaning. A toilet that overflows when only that one fixture is used is a fixture problem, and toilet repair is the page to read.

Main sewer cleaning and what it involves

A cleaning visit starts at the cleanout with a short test to see how far the cable goes and where it stops. The operator cuts through the blockage, retrieves what they can, and runs the cable again until it moves freely. A final pass with the camera or a flow test confirms water drains.

Who maintains the stretch toward the street

When every fixture and the lowest drain back up together, the blockage may sit in the line leading toward the street. The City's Utilities Division oversees sanitary sewer with 262 miles of main and 23 lift stations, but it does not state who maintains a home's service line. Habits that keep a main line clear
