## Housing age first: what Minnetonka's mix of homes means for your pipes
A house built in the 1960s or early 1970s is now well past the age when original drain lines, shutoff valves and water heaters start to fail together. Cast iron stacks scale up inside, old galvanized branches lose pressure, and rubber washers in supply valves harden. Newer pockets of town built in the 1980s and 1990s have PEX or copper supply lines and plastic drains, which fail in different ways, usually at fittings.

The practical consequence is that a repair call on an older Minnetonka street often turns into a bigger conversation. A plumber who finds a pinhole leak in a copper branch will usually check the neighboring runs too, and a camera inspection often follows a stubborn clog. If your home is on the older side, ask about [[repiping|repiping]] when the same line has failed twice.

## What Minnetonka's water supply means for fixtures and heaters
Minnetonka runs its own groundwater system. The city's drinking water report (2017 data) lists 18 wells between 405 and 575 feet deep in the Prairie du Chien-Jordan aquifer, with chlorine and fluoride added. That report does not list a hardness number, so we do not quote one.

What that means for you is simple. Spots on glassware, crusty aerators and a water heater that rumbles are all signs of mineral buildup, and the only reliable way to know your own level is to test your tap and ask the city for its annual water quality report. A [[water-heater-repair|water heater repair]] visit often starts with draining a few gallons to see how much sediment comes out.

## Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak in Minnetonka
:::prep Do these four things while you wait
### Shut off the main valve
Find the main valve near the water meter, usually in the basement, and turn it fully closed. If it will not turn, tell the dispatcher right away.
### Kill power to wet areas
If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, turn off the breaker for that area only if you can reach it dry. Stay out of standing water that touches electrical gear.
### Clear the area
Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone, and lift furniture onto blocks if you can. Open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain the lines.
### Call the 24/7 line
Describe what you see, where the shutoff is and whether any water is near electrical equipment. The dispatcher will take it from there.
:::

## A short note on safety and your insurance
Water damage claims usually move faster when you can show what happened and when. Take photos of the failed part and the wet area before you clean up, keep the old part if you can, and write down the time you shut off the water. Your policy decides what is covered, so ask your insurer about sudden burst events versus gradual leaks.

If you are weighing an expensive repair, [[call|call (952) 866-9120]] and a dispatcher will explain what the plumber would check first and how the price is set before work starts.

Plumbing work in Minnetonka is held to the same state standard as anywhere else in Minnesota, the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
