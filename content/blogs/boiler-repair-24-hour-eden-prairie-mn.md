For 24 hour boiler repair in Eden Prairie, MN, start with five safe checks: power to the boiler, the thermostat setting, the pressure gauge, the pilot or ignition light, and air trapped in the radiators. If those do not restore heat, call a plumber. Treat a gas smell, a carbon monoxide alarm, active leaking or loud banging as stop signs. In Minnesota's cold, a boiler that is out can freeze pipes within hours.

## Five checks you can do safely
These checks need no tools beyond a flashlight, and none involve opening the burner. Do them in order, and stop if anything looks unsafe.

1. **Power:** Check the boiler switch, the breaker and any nearby emergency shutoff.
2. **Thermostat:** Set it several degrees above room temperature and confirm it is on heat mode.
3. **Pressure gauge:** Most home boilers run in a fairly narrow range, and a very low reading can lock out the burner.
4. **Ignition:** Look for an indicator light or error code on the control panel.
5. **Air in the system:** If radiators are cold at the top, they may be airlocked.

## Reading the pressure gauge
The gauge, often on the front of the boiler, shows system pressure in psi. A drop to near zero often means water is leaking or the system has lost its charge. A reading far above the normal range, especially with a hissing relief valve, means the system is overheating or the expansion tank has failed.

Do not keep adding water to chase a falling gauge, since the water has to go somewhere. Mark the reading, check for wet floors around the boiler and the piping, and call if it keeps dropping. If the relief valve is discharging, keep people away from the hot spray.

## Pilot lights, ignition and error codes
Older boilers have a standing pilot, a small flame that lights the main burner. Newer ones use an electronic ignition and show a code when it fails. Look at the manual on the boiler's door or the sticker nearby for what the code means.

If the pilot is out, you may be tempted to relight it. Only do so if you are comfortable following the manufacturer's steps on the label. If you smell gas, do not try. Leave the building, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Never flip switches or use a phone indoors.

## Bleeding radiators and baseboard
Hot water systems can collect air, which keeps heat from filling a radiator or baseboard loop. Bleed valves sit at the top of cast iron radiators. Hold a cloth under the valve, open it a quarter turn with a key or small screwdriver, and close it when water, not air, spits out.

Bleeding drops system pressure, so check the gauge after. If you are not sure the boiler can refill itself, stop. A system low on water can overheat, and a plumber should refill it correctly.

## Signs that cannot wait
Some symptoms mean no heat is only the start of the problem. A CO alarm sounding means everyone should get outside right away and call 911. Do not reopen the house until responders say it is safe.

Active leaking, scorch marks around the burner, soot, a gas smell, loud banging and a relief valve that blows hot water are all reasons to switch the boiler off and call. ## Protecting pipes while the heat is out
Minnesota winters regularly bring below-zero cold, so exposed and exterior-wall pipes are at risk when a boiler stops. Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so warm air reaches the pipes. Let a faucet drip, since moving water is harder to freeze.

Close off unused rooms and run space heaters only with proper clearances and outlet use, never extension cords. Keep the garage door closed. If a pipe has frozen but not burst, warm it slowly with a hair dryer, never a flame.

## Where the water heater fits in
Many homes use the boiler for hot water as well, through an indirect tank. If you have heat but no hot water, the tank or its zone valve may be the issue, not the boiler. We also handle [[water-heater-repair|water heater repair]] for that case.

A boiler and a standard tank water heater are separate machines, so check which one you have. A tank water heater that is well past its typical service life can fail around the same cold snap.

:::faq
Q: Why is my boiler running but my house is cold?
A: Air in the loops, a stuck circulator pump or a closed zone valve can stop hot water reaching the radiators. Bleed the radiators and check the pump, or call for service.

Q: Is it safe to leave a boiler running with a small leak?
A: Not for long. A small leak can grow, drop the pressure and trigger a lockout. Switch the boiler off, catch the drips and call.

Q: How often should a boiler be serviced?
A: Once a year before the heating season is a common practice. A tune-up checks the burner, controls and safeties, and can catch a small problem before the first cold snap.

Q: What should I do if my CO alarm goes off?
A: Get everyone outside and call 911. Do not go back in to investigate. A plumber or heating technician can inspect the boiler only after responders clear the house.
