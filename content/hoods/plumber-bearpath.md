Bearpath is a private, gated community in Eden Prairie built around an 18-hole golf course designed by Jack Nicklaus. According to a Star Tribune report from 2015, construction began in 1994 on about 460 acres of former woods and fields, and Bearpath was the first gated neighborhood in Minnesota.

At that time it had 301 townhomes, twinhomes, golf villas and single-family homes. Access is limited to residents, golf club members and their guests, and the city required a public trail through the community.

Those figures come from a decade-old article, so use them as background rather than a current count. They do tell you what kind of plumbing calls to expect: large custom houses, maintenance-free villas and townhomes, and a gatehouse between the street and your front door.

## Getting a plumber through the gate
Tell the gatehouse who is coming before the visit. If you call us during a leak, give the dispatcher your address, the gate procedure and a phone number the plumber can reach once they arrive. Residents with a guest list or an access code should have it ready, because a delay at the gate can stretch a short job into a long one.

While you wait, shut off the water at the main valve if water is escaping, stop using any drain that is backing up and keep people away from wet electrical areas. Know where the main shutoff is before you need it, since a large house can have it in a basement mechanical room or a utility closet far from the kitchen.

## Bigger houses, more to maintain
A larger home usually has more bathrooms, longer pipe runs and sometimes more than one water heater or a recirculating loop. That is a general pattern, not a promise about any house in Bearpath, but it changes what you ask a plumber. A leak behind a wall in a distant wing can run a long time before a stain shows, so unexplained water-bill increases are worth checking with [[leak-detection|leak detection]].

Water heaters are the most common planned replacement in homes of every size. Ask the plumber about capacity for the number of bathrooms, venting, and whether a [[tankless-water-heater|tankless water heater]] makes sense for the layout. If one tank is failing, [[water-heater-replacement|water heater replacement]] explains how that job is scoped.

:::cards
### Single-family homes
Owners maintain their own supply lines, drains, fixtures and water heaters.
### Villas and twinhomes
Shared walls mean shared exposure, so ask your association where common lines run.
### Golf-course lots
Lawn irrigation, outdoor spigots and drainage near the course are worth checking each spring.
:::

## Water in Bearpath
Eden Prairie softens its public water to about 5 grains per gallon, and the City says most residents do not need a home softener. Whether to add a softener or a filter is a personal choice. Our [[water-softener-filtration|water softener and filtration]] page covers the options, and the City recommends that home softening not take water below about 3 grains per gallon.

:::faq
Q: Is Bearpath its own city?
A: No. Bearpath is a gated community inside Eden Prairie, in Hennepin County.

Q: Can a plumber enter Bearpath without a resident calling ahead?
A: Access is limited to residents, golf club members and guests, so the resident should notify the gatehouse before the visit and have the plumber's name and arrival details ready.

Q: Do large homes need special plumbing service?
A: The work is the same trade, but more fixtures and longer pipe runs mean more places to check. Describe the home to the dispatcher when you call.

Q: Does the golf club maintain the plumbing in my house?
A: No. The club and the association handle shared grounds and facilities. Plumbing inside your house is your responsibility unless your governing documents say otherwise.
:::
