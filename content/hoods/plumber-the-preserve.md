The Preserve sits in the southeastern part of Eden Prairie, near Anderson Lakes and Neill Lake, on land that was once the Nesbitt family farm and the Griswold farm to the south. Local reporting describes it as a "New Town" planned community that its developers announced in 1970, with the first land bought in 1971. Preserve Boulevard and Franlo Road are streets in and beside the neighborhood.

It is also one of the more unusual places to call a plumber. Eden Prairie Local News reported in 2022 that The Preserve has nearly 1,700 homes, split roughly into thirds between single-family houses, apartments, and townhomes and condominiums.

The original plan called for far more, but the city limited the project and the early 1980s recession slowed it. The result is a neighborhood where the first question on a plumbing call is often "what kind of building is it?"

## Why building type matters here
A leak in a detached house is almost always the owner's problem from the street to the faucet. In a townhome or condominium, the line between your responsibility and the association's can sit in a wall, a shared stack or a utility room. The association's governing documents usually say which parts you own, so read them before you pay for a repair, and ask your management office if a leak seems to come from a neighbor's unit.

When you call, tell the dispatcher whether you own or rent, whether the building has shared walls, and which floor you are on. Those three answers shape the plan faster than any description of the stain.

:::cards
### Single-family homes
Water heater, supply lines, drains and the sewer lateral to the main are usually yours to maintain.
### Townhomes and condominiums
Fixtures inside your unit are yours, while shared stacks, risers and outdoor lines are often the association's.
### Apartments
Report leaks to the property manager first, since the building owner controls the shutoffs and most repairs.
:::

## Homes that started going up in the early 1970s
Development in The Preserve began around 1970 and 1971, so some homes are now about half a century old. Age by itself does not tell you what is inside a wall. Pipe materials depend on when each building was built and whether any lines have been replaced, and we do not assume a problem because of a decade. A [[leak-detection|leak detection]] visit or a [[sewer-inspection|sewer camera inspection]] can show what a specific house has.

The City of Eden Prairie says most homes in town were built after plumbing code safeguards against lead and copper leaching took effect.

Water from the city system is softened to about 5 grains per gallon, so scale builds more slowly than it does in towns with unsoftened water. A tank water heater still collects sediment, and an older one is worth flushing and inspecting. If yours is leaking or failing, our pages on [[water-heater-repair|water heater repair]] and [[water-heater-replacement|water heater replacement]] explain what the work involves.

## Amenities do not cover your pipes
The Preserve's homeowners association maintains the neighborhood trails, tennis courts, community center and sand-bottom pool from its own reserve fund. That is separate from what happens inside your house. Check whether your association covers anything beyond common areas, such as exterior water spigots or a shared irrigation line, and keep a note of where your main shutoff is.

If a drain is slow in one fixture, try a plunger first. If several fixtures back up together, stop using water and call, because that pattern can point to the main line. See [[drain-cleaning|drain cleaning]] for what a plumber checks.

:::faq
Q: Is The Preserve a separate city from Eden Prairie?
A: No. The Preserve is a neighborhood in southeastern Eden Prairie, in Hennepin County. It is not an independent municipality.

Q: Who fixes a leak in a Preserve townhome or condominium?
A: It depends on where the leak starts. Pipes inside your unit are generally yours, while shared stacks and common lines are often the association's. Check your governing documents and call your management office if you are unsure.

Q: Do older Preserve homes need repiping?
A: Not automatically. Age is not proof of a problem. A plumber should look at the actual pipes, test pressure and show you what they find before recommending a replacement.

Q: Does the homeowners association handle plumbing inside my home?
A: The association maintains the shared amenities such as trails, courts and the pool. Whether it covers any building systems depends on your property type and documents.
:::
