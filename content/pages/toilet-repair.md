Most toilet trouble is slower than that. A handle that sticks, a tank that refills every few minutes or a rocking seat usually traces to one worn part, and finding that part is the whole job.

## A toilet that will not stop running
Inside the tank, a flapper seals the flush valve, a fill valve refills the tank and a float tells the fill valve when to stop. A chain connects the handle to the flapper. If any one of those wears or drifts out of adjustment, water leaks from tank to bowl and the fill valve keeps topping it up.

Check the chain first. Too tight and it holds the flapper open, too loose and the flapper drops late. A float set too high lets water spill into the overflow tube, which you hear as a thin constant hiss.

:::steps
Lift the lid: look at the water level against the mark on the overflow tube.
Check the chain: it should have only a little slack when the flapper is seated.
Press the flapper: if the running stops, the flapper or its seat is worn.
Adjust the float: lower it so the water stops below the top of the overflow tube.
Replace the parts: a fill valve or flapper kit is a short job for a plumber.
:::

## Leaks at the base of the toilet
Water around the foot of the bowl is usually the wax ring failing. That ring seals the bowl to the closet flange in the floor, and it can fail after a toilet has rocked for months, after a flange sinks, or after the closet bolts loosen. A bowl that moves when you sit on it needs attention before it needs paint.

We pull the toilet, inspect the flange for cracks or rot, set a new ring, and bolt the bowl down evenly. Overtightening cracks porcelain, so the last turns are slow.

## Flange repair
If the flange is cracked, corroded or below the finished floor, a new ring cannot seal. Repair rings or a flange extender restore the surface. A cracked tank cannot be fixed, though loose tank bolts and hardened washers can be replaced.

## When a toilet clog or overflow is an emergency
A bowl rising toward the rim is urgent. Close the valve behind it, do not flush again, and lift the float if the tank is still refilling. Repeated flushing sends more water over the rim, onto the floor and eventually into the ceiling below.

A plunger with a flange cup and a closet auger clear most single-toilet clogs. After-hours and weekend calls get the same 24/7 dispatch, and same-day appointments are offered when available if the bowl is merely slow.

:::signs Signs a toilet needs repair
### Phantom flushing
The tank refills on its own every few minutes, a classic sign the flapper is leaking into the bowl.
### Rocking on the floor
The bowl moves under your weight, which means loose closet bolts or a failing wax ring.
### Water at the tank joints
Drips at the bolts or the supply connection point to worn tank bolt washers or a loose fitting.
### A hairline crack
Any crack in the tank or bowl can open up suddenly. Shut off the supply and call.
### Weak or incomplete flush
Clogged rim jets, a low water level or a partial clog slow the siphon action.
:::

## Replacing or installing a toilet
Replace the toilet when it cracks, when parts are no longer available, or when it flushes poorly even after repair. Check the rough-in, the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, because a bowl must match it. Measure before you buy.

Comfort height seats sit a little taller and are easier for many adults. Look for the WaterSense label, the EPA's mark for water-efficient toilets. If you are adding a new bathroom, our [[plumbing-installation|plumbing installation]] team handles the supply and drain as well.

## Common toilet questions

Q: Do you handle toilet emergencies at night or on weekends in Eden Prairie?
A: Yes. The 24/7 dispatch line takes overflow and leak calls at any hour, with same-day appointments when available for less urgent repairs. Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet and stop flushing until help arrives.

Q: Why does my toilet run on its own?
A: Water is leaking from the tank into the bowl, usually past a worn flapper, or the fill valve and float are set too high. Check the chain first, then run a dye test.

Q: How do I do the dye test?
A: Add a few drops of food coloring or a dye tablet to the tank without flushing. If color reaches the bowl within a short wait, the flapper is leaking.

Q: Why is there water around the base of my toilet?
A: Most often the wax ring has failed or the bolts are loose. Cracked porcelain and condensation on the tank can also leave a puddle.

Q: Should I keep flushing a toilet that is clogged?
A: No. Shut the valve behind the toilet and use a flange plunger. Each extra flush adds water that can overflow.
