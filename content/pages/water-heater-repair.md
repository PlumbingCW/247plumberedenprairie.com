## When a leaking tank is an emergency
Water pooling under a tank is urgent because it can ruin drywall, flooring and stored boxes quickly, and an electric unit with water around it is also an electrical hazard. If water is spraying or running steadily, close the cold supply, cut power, and open a hot tap upstairs so the tank does not keep refilling and pushing water out.

Overnight and weekend calls are routine for this problem. A small seep can wait for a same-day appointment when available, but a steady stream should not.

## Where a water heater leak comes from
- **Inlet and outlet connections:** loose or corroded nipples and flex connectors drip at the top of the tank.
- **T&P valve:** a leaking temperature and pressure relief valve may be worn, or it may be reacting to high pressure or heat.
- **Drain valve:** a plastic spigot at the bottom can weep after a flush or crack with age.
- **Condensation:** a cold tank in a humid basement or a high-efficiency unit can sweat or drip from its condensate line.
- **Tank body:** water seeping from the jacket or the base means the steel liner has rusted through.

Dry the area and watch which fitting gets wet. A fitting leak is repairable. A leak from the tank itself is not, and our [[water-heater-replacement|replacement team]] can walk you through next steps.

## Reading the signs before you call
:::signs What the unit is telling you
### Puddle at the base
Water under the tank with dry fittings above usually points to a failed liner or a bad drain valve.
### Popping or rumbling
Sediment baked onto the bottom or an element traps steam bubbles, which then burst as they rise.
### Rusty hot water
Reddish water from hot taps only, not cold, suggests the anode rod is spent and the tank is corroding.
### Discharge pipe dripping
Water from the T&P pipe can mean excess pressure or a valve that no longer seats.
:::

## No hot water: what usually fails
A tank that makes no hot water at all, or only lukewarm water, has a short list of likely causes, and the fuel type narrows it fast.

On a gas unit, a pilot that will not stay lit often means a worn thermocouple or a dirty flame sensor, and a newer unit with an electronic igniter may show a blink code. On an electric unit, check the breaker first, then the red reset button on the upper thermostat. A failed upper element leaves you with no hot water, while a failed lower element gives you a short supply that runs out quickly.

Sediment also steals capacity. Minerals settle at the bottom, insulate the burner or element from the water, and the tank cycles longer for less heat. The City softens its tap water to about 5 grains per gallon, so scale builds slowly here compared with towns on unsoftened well water. Sediment still collects over time, and a tank that has never been flushed will show it.

## Gas, electric and heat pump repairs
Each type breaks in its own way, and the repair list differs.

On gas units, plumbers replace thermocouples, gas control valves, igniters and flame sensors, and inspect the flue and draft hood for backdrafting or blockage. Power-vent models add a blower and pressure switch that can fail. Electric units need new heating elements, thermostats or high-limit switches, and a check on wiring and breaker size.

Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor, fan, air filter and condensate drain. A clogged filter or blocked drain line is a common reason they fall back to expensive resistance heating. All tank types depend on the anode rod, so replacing it in a middle-aged tank can extend its life, provided the tank is still sound.

## If you smell gas
Natural gas and propane smell like rotten eggs. Do not flip switches, use a phone or light anything inside. Leave the building first, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Once the utility confirms the building is safe, we can inspect the burner, vent and gas control valve.

## Why Eden Prairie tanks age the way they do
Most homes here date from the 1970s onward, so many water heaters sit in basements next to the meter, and a leak there can go unnoticed for days. The City notes that odors can have many causes, including an aging water heater, so a rotten-egg or musty smell from hot water only is worth a check of the tank and anode rod.

A typical tank lasts roughly eight to twelve years, so a leaking tank in that range is usually past repair.

Repairs on a water heater follow the manufacturer's guidelines, and the piping, relief valve and shutoffs fall under the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
