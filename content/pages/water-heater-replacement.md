## A failed tank versus a leaking fitting
Steel tanks rust from the inside. Once the liner is breached, water seeps through the insulation and drips from the jacket or base, and no patch will hold.

To check, dry every pipe connection with a rag and watch for 15 minutes or so. If water returns at the top fittings, the T&P valve or the drain valve, a repair may be enough. If the base stays wet while the fittings stay dry, or the tank sweats rust-colored water, the tank is finished. Our [[water-heater-repair|repair technicians]] will confirm before recommending a new unit.

## When a failed water heater is an emergency
A flooding tank, a scalding-hot relief valve discharge or an electric unit sitting in standing water cannot wait for morning. Call right away, shut off the supply, and open one hot tap upstairs so the tank stops pushing water out.

A slow weep on a tank that still heats is less urgent, and we can often book a same-day appointment when available. Overnight and weekend calls reach the same dispatch line.

## No hot water and what it means for the tank
Many no-hot-water calls end in a thermostat, element, thermocouple or igniter, and those are repairs. Replacement enters the picture when the tank is old, leaking, or the fix costs close to the price of a new unit.

Age matters. A tank in the eight to twelve year range has used up most of its anode rod and liner. Spending on a gas control valve for that tank often buys little, so we explain both options and let you choose.

## Choosing a size and fuel type
Capacity should match the household and the busiest hour of use, not just the number of bathrooms. Two adults usually do well with a smaller tank, while a family with teenagers, a soaking tub and a dishwasher running at the same time needs more recovery and storage.

Fuel type changes the job. Gas replacement needs a safe flue and combustion air. Electric needs the right breaker and wire size. Moving from atmospheric to power vent, or from gas to a heat pump unit, changes the venting, condensate and electrical work.

## What the swap involves
:::steps
Shut down and drain: the supply, power or gas are closed and the old tank is drained to a floor drain or outside.
Disconnect and remove: water lines, gas or electrical, vent and T&P discharge are disconnected, and the old unit is hauled away.
Set the new unit: the tank sits on a drain pan or in a protected area with a drain, level and secure.
Reconnect with new fittings: flex connectors, dielectric fittings and a shutoff valve replace worn parts.
Add expansion control: an expansion tank is added when the system is closed, and the T&P discharge pipe is routed safely.
Fill, purge and test: the tank is filled, air is bled, gas joints are leak-tested and the burner or elements are checked.
:::

Most swaps finish in a single visit, and same-day replacement is available when stock and schedules allow. Larger scopes such as new vent runs or relocating the unit are part of a broader [[plumbing-installation|plumbing installation]] job.
