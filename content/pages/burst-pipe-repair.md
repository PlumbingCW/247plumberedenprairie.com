If you need burst pipe repair in Eden Prairie, treat it as an emergency and move fast. Shut off the main valve at the meter, open a faucet lower in the house to drain the lines, and keep people away from wet electrical areas, including outlets, panels and lights under a dripping ceiling. Then call our 24/7 dispatch line, where licensed plumbers answer around the clock.

## When a burst pipe is an emergency
Water spraying, a ceiling bulging with trapped water, a sheared fitting or a pipe that has split along a seam all need an immediate shutoff and a call. So does any pipe that froze and now drips as it thaws, because the ice may have been holding a crack closed. Nights, weekends and holidays are when winter pipes tend to fail, so after-hours dispatch is the normal way these calls come in.

A single pinhole drip at a fitting is less urgent. Shut the line, set a bucket under it and book a same-day appointment when available.

## Find the shutoff before you need it
Most Eden Prairie meters sit inside the house, usually in the basement near the water heater. The City says most meters have two shutoff valves, one on each side, and a few have one. Closing the valve on the house side of the meter stops water to the whole home.

The curb stop is a different valve. It is street-side and lets the City shut off service to your home, and the City will adjust it to ground level and replace the cap at no charge if you keep it uncovered. For emergencies, the City says crews try to notify residents door-to-door before shutting off water. Do not wait for that if your own valve will stop the flood.

## Why pipes burst in Minnesota homes
Minnesota winters regularly bring below-zero cold, and water expands when it freezes. A pipe in an exterior wall, unheated garage or crawlspace can freeze solid, and the leak may appear only when the thaw starts.

Most Eden Prairie homes date from the 1970s onward, so many have old copper joints, aging fittings and long runs along outside walls. Corrosion and one hard freeze together are the usual recipe for a failure.

:::signs What a failing pipe looks like
### Water where it should not be
Wet drywall, dripping light fixtures or a spreading stain on a ceiling.
### A sudden loss of pressure
Faucets sputter or run weak across the house.
### Frost or bulging on a pipe
A frosted run in a cold wall or a swollen pipe is about to split.
### Hissing, banging or running sounds
Water noise with everything shut off.
### A meter that will not stop
A spinning dial with all fixtures closed means water is escaping.
:::

## Cracked and broken pipe repair
This is the core of the job. A cracked or broken pipe is cut out and replaced, since patching a split with tape or clamps is a short-term fix at best. The plumber closes the water, drains the line, finds the full extent of the damage, and cuts back to sound pipe on both sides.

The section is replaced with new pipe and fittings, soldered, pressed or crimped depending on the material. Pressure comes back slowly and every joint is checked for weeping. If a freeze caused the crack, we check the rest of the run for weak spots.

## Repair options and their trade-offs
:::table
Option | Best for | Trade-off
Cut-out and replace a section | A single split in otherwise sound pipe | Needs access to both ends of the cut
Repair coupling | Quick fix on straight runs | Fine for a spot, but not for corroded pipe
Replace a whole run | Several failures on one line | More opening in walls, longer visit
Repipe the branch | Aging or repeatedly failing pipe | Highest effort, fewest repeat calls
:::

## Copper, drain and other pipe types
Copper pipe splits most often at a frozen elbow or a corroded joint, and repair means sweating in a new section or using a press fitting. A cracked drain pipe leaks only when water flows through it, then the bad section is cut out and new pipe joined with proper slope.

A plain pipe leak at a fitting may only need the joint remade. For a slow drip that is not a full burst, [[leak-detection|leak detection]] confirms where the water is coming from before anything is cut.

## Controlling the water and the damage
Soaked drywall and ceilings hold water, so a small relief hole in a bulging ceiling drains it before the drywall fails. Move furniture and lift rugs. We stop the water and fix the pipe, but drying and rebuilding are other trades.

## Frozen pipes: thaw them safely
If a pipe is frozen but not yet split, open the faucet it feeds so melting water has a place to go. Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or heating pad, working from the faucet side back toward the frozen spot. Never use an open flame, a torch or a space heater near wood, because pipes in walls cause house fires this way.

If the pipe has already started leaking, close the main valve and call. The City Utilities Division handles frozen-pipe advice on weekdays from 7 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., and after that and on weekends it directs reports to the Police Department.

Pipes that freeze are usually in exterior walls, sink cabinets on outside walls, garages and crawlspaces.

## What drives the cost of a burst pipe repair
The price depends on where the pipe is and how much needs replacing, not on how bad the flood looked. We give you a clear price before work starts.

:::factors
Pipe location: exposed basement runs are faster than lines behind drywall or above a finished ceiling.
Pipe material: copper, PEX and galvanized each need different fittings and tools.
Length of damage: one split is a cut-out, while a long frozen run may need a replacement section.
Access and cleanup: opening a wall or ceiling adds work, and drying is a separate trade.
Time of day: night and weekend calls involve after-hours dispatch.
:::
