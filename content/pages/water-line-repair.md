## When a water line problem is an emergency
Water bubbling up in the yard, a lawn that is suddenly soft, a basement wall running wet where the line enters, or a house with no water at all can mean the service line has broken. These are emergencies, and they happen on nights and weekends as often as weekdays. A smaller weep that shows only on the meter can wait for a same-day appointment when available, as long as the main valve is closed when no one needs water.

## The service line, in plain terms
The water service line is the pipe from the street to the meter in your house. The City says property owners maintain all piping on their property, including the underground water service to the property line. So if that pipe leaks, finding and repairing it is the owner's job. For where the City side begins, ask the Utilities Division.

The City also says water meters in Eden Prairie are inside the home, usually in the basement near the water heater. That means the service line runs under your yard, through the foundation and up to the meter, and every leak in that stretch is ahead of the meter's shutoff valves.

## Signs of a service line leak
:::signs Water line leak signs
### A wet patch in the yard
Ground that stays soft, green or pooled in dry weather often sits above a leaking line.
### Dropped water pressure
All fixtures run weaker at once, not just one tap.
### A meter that keeps running
Close every tap and appliance. If the dial moves, water is escaping.
### The sound of running water
A hiss near the foundation wall where the line enters the house.
### A sudden jump in the bill
Usage well above the City's typical figures with no change in habits.
:::

The City method for confirming a leak is simple. Record the meter reading at bedtime, use no water overnight (with irrigation and water-using appliances off), read it in the morning, and a changed reading means a leak. For a household of three, the City says typical use is about 22,000 gallons per quarter. If you are far above that, call.

## Buried line leaks and how they are found
A buried leak does not always surface. The water can follow gravel or soil and drain away, so the yard stays normal while the meter spins. We isolate the line, listen along it with ground microphones, trace its route and mark the leak position. A camera or tracer gas helps when the line is accessible. Our [[leak-detection|leak detection]] page explains each method in more detail.

Call 811 (Gopher State One Call) before any digging so other underground utilities are marked. Do not dig near the line yourself, because gas, electric and communications lines may run close by.

## Repair or replace the line
:::table
Choice | Best when | Trade-off
Spot repair | One leak in an otherwise sound line | Cheapest in effort, but other weak spots may remain
Open trench replacement | Old, corroded or repeatedly leaking lines | Digs up lawn or driveway
Trenchless replacement | Long runs under landscaping or pavement | Needs entry and exit pits and suitable soil
:::

A spot repair makes sense when the pipe is sound and only a joint has failed. Replacement makes sense when the line is old, corroded or has leaked more than once, since each repair opens the ground again. Trenchless methods pull or burst a new line along the old path and disturb less surface, but they depend on the pipe's condition and the soil. We explain the choice on site and give you a clear price before work starts.

## Low water pressure at the whole house
Low pressure is the second most common reason people look at their water line. A pressure reducing valve near the meter can fail or drift out of adjustment. A partly closed valve, a corroded galvanized section or a hidden leak can all cut flow, and a problem on the City side can too.

To narrow it down, check whether pressure is low at hot and cold taps, at one fixture or all, and whether the water was off recently. If the drop appeared suddenly, a leak is more likely than a worn valve. Persistent low pressure in an older home may lead to [[repiping|repiping]].

## New water services and plumbing installation
A new water service is the pipe and valves that bring water from the main to a new house, addition or outbuilding. [[plumbing-installation|Plumbing installation]] covers the connection to the meter, shutoff valves and the supply lines inside. Permits and inspections vary by job, so ask the Eden Prairie building department what applies. New construction follows the same pattern: a trench, a properly bedded line and a pressure test before the ground is closed.

## What drives the cost of water line repair
The price follows the length of the line and what sits on top of it. A short repair in open soil is a different job from a long run under a driveway. We give you a clear price before work starts.

:::factors
Depth and length: deeper or longer lines take more digging or more trenchless setup.
Surface above the line: lawn, concrete, landscaping and pavement each change the restoration work.
Pipe condition: sound pipe takes a spot repair, while brittle pipe pushes toward replacement.
Method chosen: open trench and trenchless work use different equipment and crews.
Access and permits: tight access and permit steps add time to the schedule.
:::

## What to have ready when you call
Know where the main valve is and whether it is closed. Note where the wet spot or pressure drop appears, and what the meter reads. If you can, mark the spot in the yard and keep pets and children away from soft ground.
