Snake, jet or camera first: how to decide

A cable is the right first move for one fixture with a soft clog near the trap. It is the wrong move when the blockage keeps returning, because a cable punches a hole through debris and leaves the walls coated.

Jetting sends pressurized water through a hose and scours the pipe wall, which suits grease, scale and silt. A camera goes in first when you do not know what you are fighting, when several fixtures are involved, or when a cable has already failed once. Our hydro jetting page explains when water beats steel.

When a clogged drain is an emergency

Some clogs can wait until morning. These cannot:

  • Sewage or gray water coming up through a floor drain or basement shower
  • Every fixture on the lowest level backing up at once
  • Water spreading toward finished flooring, a furnace or a panel

If any of those match, turn off the washer, stop running water, and call. We take after-hours and weekend calls like any other, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Signs the drain needs attention

What the pipe is telling you

Slow in one fixture

Usually a hair or soap clog in the trap or the first few feet of branch line.

Slow in several fixtures

Two or three fixtures dragging together point past the traps to the branch or main line.

Gurgling after a flush

Air pulled through a trap means the line is partly blocked downstream.

Water in the shower while the washer runs

Laundry water has nowhere to go, so it rises at the lowest open drain in the house.

Tubs, showers, laundry drains and floor drains

Bath and shower drains clog with hair wound around the stopper or crossbar, held together by soap film. A drain claw often finishes the job, but deep standing water with a clean stopper means the clog sits lower and needs a machine.

Laundry drains carry lint and detergent residue, and a standpipe that overflows during the spin cycle usually means a lint-packed trap arm. A floor drain that smells is more often a dried-out trap seal than a blockage. Basement floor drains sit at the low point of the house, so they show a deeper problem first.

Unclogging a drain without making it worse

A plunger with a good seal works on tubs and sinks if you block the overflow opening first. Stop there. Chemical openers sit in standing water, can soften old pipe joints and burn the skin of whoever works on the line next.

Snaking and cabling: when each tool earns its place

A hand auger handles sink and tub traps. A drum machine with a cutter head clears branch lines and chews through roots and soft scale. Water is the better call for repeat grease clogs. A camera belongs first when you suspect a break, a sag or a foreign object, because the picture tells you whether to cut, flush or dig. For that decision see sewer inspection.

Kitchen sink clog

The kitchen drain takes grease that cools into a wax lining, coffee grounds, rice that swells and fibrous peels. The clog usually forms in the trap arm or at the first elbow in the wall, where flow slows down.

First rule out the disposal. If it hums but does not spin, the jam is in the unit, and our garbage disposal page covers that case. If both basins are full and the disposal runs freely, the blockage is in the drain line.

Remove the trap with a bucket below it, clear it, then run a hand auger into the wall pipe. If a double sink backs up on both sides, a machine with a cutter head clears the wall and the branch beyond.

Grease is the repeat offender. Pour it into a can and bin it, and wipe pans before washing, or the same clog returns every few months.

Why drains here clog: house age and the line to the street

Per the City, most Eden Prairie homes date from the 1970s onward, and a few older farm-era buildings remain. That mix matters underground. Newer plastic lines rarely scale but can sag, while older cast iron branches narrow with rough scale that catches paper and grease.

Local note

The line toward the street

When every fixture and the lowest drain in the house back up together, the blockage may sit in the line leading toward the street rather than inside your plumbing. The City's Utilities Division oversees sanitary sewer and does not publish who maintains a home's service line, so ask the Utilities Division which stretch is yours. See also sewer line cleaning.

When the drain line itself leaks

A drain line that leaks inside a wall or under a slab behaves differently from a clog. You find damp drywall or a musty patch in the basement floor that appears only when water runs. If the spot dries between uses, suspect the drain line rather than a supply pipe. A camera and a flow test locate the break, and sewer line repair covers the options.
