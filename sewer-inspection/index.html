Sewer Inspection in Eden Prairie, MN

A camera inspection shows what is happening inside your sewer line before you pay to clean, line or dig. Here is how it works and what the findings mean.

A sewer inspection puts a small camera inside the line that carries waste out of your Eden Prairie, MN home, so you can see its condition instead of guessing from symptoms. It is the planned, low-pressure way to learn whether a pipe is sound, rooted, sagging or cracked. If you have an active backup instead, our 24/7 dispatch takes that call first.

How a camera inspection works

A technician feeds a flexible push cable with a camera head into the line through a cleanout, a pulled toilet or a roof vent. The head carries a light and is self-leveling, so the image stays upright even as the cable twists through bends. A transmitter in the head lets a surface locator mark the spot and depth of anything of interest.

The technician watches a monitor as the head moves, pausing at joints and defects. When something is worth noting, they mark the position on the surface so a repair crew can find it without guessing. Our sewer line cleaning page covers the clearing that sometimes comes first.

What the camera can find

Common findings

Bellies

A sag where the pipe has settled and holds water, which collects paper and solids.

Offsets and separated joints

Two pipe sections that no longer line up, leaving a lip that catches debris.

Root intrusion

Fine roots entering at a joint, or a thick mat filling the pipe.

Cracks and breaks

Longitudinal or circumferential cracks, sometimes with soil visible behind them.

Scale and corrosion

Rough buildup inside old cast iron that narrows the pipe.

Collapsed sections and tie-ins

A crushed length, or a side connection that was cut in badly.

When to schedule one

You do not need a backup to justify an inspection. These are the situations where a camera pays off:

  • Buying a home, especially an older one, before closing
  • Repeated backups or slow drains that return after cleaning
  • Before lining, digging or any repair, so the work is based on a picture
  • After a storm or ground movement that could have shifted pipe
  • Before landscaping, tree planting or a driveway project over the line

Per the City, most Eden Prairie homes date from the 1970s onward, with some older farm-era buildings. That mix means a home in the neighborhood might have a cast iron or clay line, while a newer one probably has plastic. The age of the house tells you what to expect, and the camera tells you what is real.

Access: the cleanout matters

A cleanout is the cap on a pipe at the foundation or in the yard. With one, the technician can enter the line without moving fixtures. Without one, they may have to pull a toilet, which takes longer and can limit how far the camera reaches.

If you are scheduling an inspection, find the cleanout in advance and clear a path to it. If the line is full of roots or grease, a cable or jet pass may be needed first so the lens can see. That is where hydro jetting often comes in.

What you can expect to see and ask for

Ask to watch the monitor while the camera runs. Ask for the recording or still images of every defect, along with a note of where each one sits and roughly how deep the pipe is. A good report ties the pictures to the locator marks so you can compare them later.

Keep the footage. If a later repair, a buyer or an insurer asks what condition the line was in, the recording answers it.

Inspection versus cleaning

Cleaning removes a blockage, and an inspection tells you why it formed. A cleaned line can still be cracked, bellied or rooted at a joint, so the same clog returns. For everyday sink and tub clogs, drain cleaning is the right visit, and the camera is the follow-up when clogs repeat.

Who maintains which stretch

Local note

The Eden Prairie sewer system

The City's Utilities Division oversees water, sanitary sewer and storm sewer, including 262 miles of sanitary main. It does not state who owns or maintains a home's sewer service line. If your inspection finds a defect toward the street, ask the Utilities Division who maintains that section before you commission a repair.
